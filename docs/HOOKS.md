# The Hook Library — All 47 Formulas

Two sections: **21 long-form** formulas (YouTube; the hook is the first
15 seconds) and **26 short-form** formulas (Reels / TikTok / Shorts; the
hook is the first 2 seconds). Every entry carries the shape, a written
example, the on-screen card, the failure mode, and when to reach for it.

Notes on this reference:

- On-screen cards are six words or fewer, with no punctuation they do not
  need, because they are read before they are heard.
- The long-form table in SKILL.md defines shape and example for each
  formula; the on-screen cards for long-form entries below are derived for
  this reference under the same six-word rule.
- Selection method (SKILL.md): pick **three formulas that genuinely fit
  the idea** (never three rewrites of one), write spoken + on-screen for
  each, score all three on the 5-property panel, and show the ranking.
  Never hand over one hook.

---

## Long-form hook formulas (1-21)

A long-form hook does three jobs or the video leaks: **confirm the click
the title promised, open a question the viewer cannot close, prove the
payoff exists.** A hook that only does the third is a summary. Rules:
confirm the title in the first sentence; one idea; specific beats big;
show the artifact inside 20 seconds if you promised one; never open with
who you are.

### 1. The Statistic
**Shape:** a number the viewer did not know, stated flat, then the
consequence. **Example:** "97% of people who start a channel quit before
video 30. Here is what the other 3% do." **On-screen:** `97% QUIT BEFORE
VIDEO 30`. **Failure mode:** dies if the number is vague or unverifiable —
say the source out loud. **When to use:** you hold a real, checkable
number whose consequence matters to the viewer.

### 2. Someone Else's Result
**Shape:** a named person's outcome, then the mechanism. **Example:**
"This channel went 400 to 200,000 subs in seven months on one format. I
mapped it." **On-screen:** `400 -> 200,000 SUBS`. **Failure mode:** do not
invent the number — if you cannot name the channel, use a different
formula. **When to use:** you can name and link the source, and the proof
exists publicly.

### 3. The Mistake
**Shape:** name the error the viewer is probably making right now.
**Example:** "Your first 30 seconds are why nobody finishes your videos."
**On-screen:** `IT'S YOUR FIRST 30 SECONDS`. **Failure mode:** only works
if the mistake is common and the viewer can check it in one second.
**When to use:** the error is common, invisible, and instantly checkable.

### 4. Contrarian Flip
**Shape:** state the accepted advice, then reject it. **Example:**
"Everyone says post daily. I posted weekly for a year and tripled the
channel." **On-screen:** `POST DAILY? NO.` **Failure mode:** needs
evidence in the next 15 seconds or it reads as contrarian for its own
sake. **When to use:** you have evidence against the consensus, not just
an opinion.

### 5. The Reveal
**Shape:** promise a specific thing will be shown, then show it.
**Example:** "I'm going to show you the exact prompt, on screen, that
wrote this video." **On-screen:** `THE EXACT PROMPT`. **Failure mode:**
you have to actually show it, early — a reveal at 8:00 is a retention
cliff at 1:00. **When to use:** you have a concrete artifact — a prompt, a
template, a file — you can show almost immediately.

### 6. The Superlative
**Shape:** the single best/worst/fastest of a category; earn it by naming
what it beat. **Example:** "The fastest way to turn one video into thirty
Shorts." **On-screen:** `ONE VIDEO -> 30 SHORTS`. **Failure mode:**
superlatives are cheap — earn it by naming what it beat. **When to use:**
the claim is genuinely the top of a category and the comparison is
nameable.

### 7. The Clock
**Shape:** bound the payoff in time; the clock must be true. **Example:**
"In the next six minutes you'll have a title, thumbnail and script."
**On-screen:** `DONE IN 6 MINUTES`. **Failure mode:** the clock has to be
true — overrun it and the next video's hook is not believed. **When to
use:** the payoff genuinely fits inside the stated time.

### 8. I Tried It
**Shape:** first-person experiment with a stated cost. **Example:** "I let
an AI run my channel for 30 days. I didn't touch the uploads."
**On-screen:** `AI RAN MY CHANNEL. 30 DAYS.` **Failure mode:** needs a
real cost — time, money, risk — or there is no stake. **When to use:** you
ran a real experiment and paid a real, stateable price.

### 9. The Question
**Shape:** ask the exact question the viewer typed into search.
**Example:** "Why do your videos die at 30 seconds when the topic is
fine?" **On-screen:** `WHY VIDEOS DIE AT 0:30`. **Failure mode:** must be
the viewer's question, not yours — pull it from your own comments.
**When to use:** the question matches a real search or a repeated comment.

### 10. Before and After
**Shape:** two states, one cut between them; needs a visual. **Example:**
"This was my thumbnail. This is it now. Same video, 4x the clicks."
**On-screen:** `THEN / NOW`. **Failure mode:** only works with a visual —
do not use it on a talking-head-only video. **When to use:** you have both
states on tape and one clean cut between them.

### 11. The Teardown
**Shape:** take a real thing apart in public; name what and how many.
**Example:** "I pulled the nine most-viewed AI videos of the month and
broke down the titles." **On-screen:** `9 VIDEOS, TORN DOWN`. **Failure
mode:** name what you pulled and how many — a teardown of one example is
an anecdote. **When to use:** you have a real corpus of N items and a
pattern worth teaching.

### 12. The Stack
**Shape:** two named tools combined into one outcome. **Example:** "Claude
plus your Studio export gives next week's schedule in one prompt."
**On-screen:** `CLAUDE + STUDIO EXPORT`. **Failure mode:** both tools must
be nameable and the outcome must need both. **When to use:** the
combination itself is the trick.

### 13. The Warning
**Shape:** a cost the viewer is about to pay; reserve it — a small cost is
a lie. **Example:** "Don't upload another video until you check this one
setting." **On-screen:** `CHECK THIS ONE SETTING`. **Failure mode:** if
the cost is small the hook is a lie — reserve it. **When to use:** the
cost is real and specific, and the fix is one step away.

### 14. The List
**Shape:** a counted set, with the count in the first line. **Example:**
"Seven things I'd do differently if I started a channel in 2026."
**On-screen:** `7 THINGS I'D CHANGE`. **Failure mode:** the count must be
exact and every item must be different in kind. **When to use:** you
genuinely have N distinct items, each different in kind.

### 15. The Receipt
**Shape:** show the artifact first, explain second; a described receipt is
not a receipt. **Example:** "This is the analytics page. It did 40% of the
channel's watch time." **On-screen:** `REAL ANALYTICS PAGE`. **Failure
mode:** requires a real screenshot — a described receipt is not a receipt.
**When to use:** the artifact — dashboard, screenshot, file — is actually
on screen.

### 16. The Insider
**Shape:** information from inside a system; never claim access you lack.
**Example:** "YouTube tells you which videos underperform. Almost nobody
opens the report." **On-screen:** `THE REPORT NOBODY OPENS`. **Failure
mode:** never claim insider access you do not have. **When to use:** you
know a report, feature or mechanic most users never open.

### 17. The Impossible Claim
**Shape:** state something that sounds untrue, then prove it fast.
**Example:** "You can plan a week of videos without opening a single
editor." **On-screen:** `A WEEK, NO EDITOR`. **Failure mode:** the proof
has to start within 20 seconds or it reads as clickbait. **When to use:**
the claim sounds untrue but is demonstrable almost immediately.

### 18. The Comparison
**Shape:** two named options, one winner; say the loser out loud.
**Example:** "I wrote the same video three ways. One held 62%, two
didn't." **On-screen:** `3 SCRIPTS. 1 WINNER.` **Failure mode:** say the
losing option out loud — a comparison with no loser is an advert. **When
to use:** you ran both options and can name the loser.

### 19. The Origin
**Shape:** where a result actually came from; works once per channel.
**Example:** "Every video on this channel starts in the same 12-line file.
Here it is." **On-screen:** `THE 12-LINE FILE`. **Failure mode:** works
once per channel — it is a reveal about you, and you only have one.
**When to use:** the origin is singular and genuinely yours.

### 20. The Deadline
**Shape:** something is changing on a real, checkable date. **Example:**
"The old description format stops mattering this month." **On-screen:**
`CHANGES THIS MONTH`. **Failure mode:** only use with a real, checkable
date — the fastest formula to lose trust with. **When to use:** a real
platform, policy or seasonal change exists and matters to the viewer.

### 21. The Direct Address
**Shape:** name the exact viewer in the first six words; narrow beats
broad. **Example:** "If you have under a thousand subs, this is the only
video you need this week." **On-screen:** `UNDER 1,000 SUBS?`. **Failure
mode:** narrow beats broad — "if you make videos" addresses nobody.
**When to use:** you can name the exact sliver of viewer in six words.

## Short-form hook formulas (1-26)

Rules: write the spoken line and the on-screen line separately; no setup —
the video starts at the sentence you would normally reach at second six;
say the number; the first frame is a hook too; if the hook would work for
anyone else in the niche it is not your hook; deliver the promise before
the halfway mark.

### 1. Cost Confession
**Shape:** {Amount} is what {one mistake} cost me. **Example:** "$18,000
is what one missing clause cost me." **On-screen:** `$18,000 MISTAKE`.
**Failure mode:** a fake cost — "it cost me my peace of mind" is not a
number, and everybody can hear that. **When to use:** trust, fast; people
finish the videos where you are the one who looks bad.

### 2. Negative Command
**Shape:** Stop {common action}. Do {alternative} instead. **Example:**
"Stop posting three times a day. Post twice and reply to every comment
for an hour." **On-screen:** `STOP POSTING DAILY`. **Failure mode:**
telling people to stop doing something nobody does — the habit has to be
one the viewer recognises in themselves. **When to use:** saves and
arguments; the imperative gets attention a statement does not.

### 3. Nobody Tells You
**Shape:** Nobody tells you that {uncomfortable truth}. **Example:**
"Nobody tells you that your first 30 reels are supposed to flop."
**On-screen:** `YOUR FIRST 30 WILL FLOP`. **Failure mode:** picking a
truth everybody already says — if the comments agree before you finish,
it was not a secret. **When to use:** new audiences; it positions you as
the person who says the quiet part.

### 4. The Replacement
**Shape:** {Thing} replaced {expensive thing} for {price}. **Example:**
"This $12 tool replaced the editor I was paying $2,000 a month."
**On-screen:** `$2,000 -> $12`. **Failure mode:** overstating the
replacement — if it replaced 60% of the job, say 60%, or the comments will
say it for you. **When to use:** demos and tool videos; the price gap does
the work.

### 5. Time Collapse
**Shape:** This used to take {long}. It now takes {short}. **Example:**
"Proposals used to take me five hours. They take twenty minutes now."
**On-screen:** `5 HOURS -> 20 MIN`. **Failure mode:** a ratio nobody
believes — 40 hours to 4 seconds gets treated as a lie even when it is
true. **When to use:** process videos where the payoff is speed; reads
instantly on screen.

### 6. The Receipt
**Shape:** I {did thing} for {N days}. Here are the actual numbers.
**Example:** "I posted one reel a day for 60 days. Here is what it did to
my DMs." **On-screen:** `60 DAYS. REAL NUMBERS.` **Failure mode:** not
showing the screen — a numbers video with no screen recording is just a
numbers claim. **When to use:** experiments, paired with the real
dashboard, which is the whole value.

### 7. Wrong Way, Right Way
**Shape:** You're doing {thing} wrong, and it's not your fault. {Fix}.
**Example:** "You are writing captions wrong, and it is not your fault.
Instagram hides everything after line one." **On-screen:** `YOUR CAPTIONS
ARE CUT`. **Failure mode:** skipping the absolution — "you are doing it
wrong" on its own makes people leave rather than learn. **When to use:**
teaching a common error without sounding like an insult.

### 8. Insider Leak
**Shape:** I spent {N years} inside {thing}. Here's what we never said out
loud. **Example:** "I spent six years selling agency retainers. Here is
what we never put in the proposal." **On-screen:** `6 YEARS INSIDE`.
**Failure mode:** promising a secret and delivering the brochure — the
comments are merciless about this one. **When to use:** authority without
a credential slide; the years must be real.

### 9. The Steal
**Shape:** Steal this {artifact}. It took me {time} to get right.
**Example:** "Steal this four-line follow-up. It took me two years and a
lot of dead leads." **On-screen:** `STEAL THIS`. **Failure mode:** the
artifact not being on screen — if they cannot screenshot it, there is
nothing to steal. **When to use:** saves and sends, the two metrics that
move reach the most.

### 10. If This, Then Watch
**Shape:** If you {specific situation}, this {short time} fixes it.
**Example:** "If your reels get views and no followers, the next 40
seconds fix it." **On-screen:** `VIEWS BUT NO FOLLOWERS?`. **Failure
mode:** a situation so broad it qualifies nobody — "if you want to grow"
is not a situation. **When to use:** qualifying hard; fewer people stay
and the ones who do are the ones who buy.

### 11. Numbered With A Favourite
**Shape:** {N} {things} that {outcome}. Number {k} is the one nobody uses.
**Example:** "Five caption edits that double saves. Number four is the one
nobody does." **On-screen:** `5 EDITS. #4 IS THE ONE.` **Failure mode:**
N above seven — nobody believes you have nine good ones, and you do not.
**When to use:** retention through the middle; the named favourite gives
people a reason to stay past three.

### 12. The Objection
**Shape:** "{Objection they actually say}." Fine. Here's what works
anyway. **Example:** "I don't want to be on camera." Fine. Here is the
faceless version that still sells. **On-screen:** `"I HATE BEING ON
CAMERA"`. **Failure mode:** inventing the objection — pull it verbatim
from a DM or a comment, including the bad grammar. **When to use:**
selling to the people who already said no once.

### 13. Before/After, On Screen
**Shape:** {Old thing}. {New thing}. {One sentence on the lever}.
**Example:** "This was my profile in March. This is it now. I changed the
name field, nothing else." **On-screen:** `MARCH / NOW`. **Failure mode:**
three levers — name one thing that changed or the transformation stops
being believable. **When to use:** anything visual; the cut between the
two frames is the hook and the words are secondary.

### 14. The Callout
**Shape:** {Specific group}, this is the one you've been ignoring.
**Example:** "Photographers charging under $2,000: this is the pricing
page you have been avoiding." **On-screen:** `PHOTOGRAPHERS UNDER $2K`.
**Failure mode:** a group so large it is not a callout — "entrepreneurs"
is not a group. **When to use:** cutting a broad audience down to the one
that converts; send rate goes up because people tag each other.

### 15. The Flop Record
**Shape:** My first {N} {attempts} did nothing. Here's what changed on
{N+1}. **Example:** "My first 47 reels averaged 300 views. Number 48 did
400,000. One thing was different." **On-screen:** `47 FLOPS, THEN THIS`.
**Failure mode:** making the change sound like luck — if the answer is "I
got lucky", there is no video. **When to use:** people at the start, which
is most of the audience; high comment rate.

### 16. The Verbatim Question
**Shape:** "{Question exactly as asked}" - I get this every week.
**Example:** "How do I post consistently without hating it?" I get this
one every week. **On-screen:** `ASKED EVERY WEEK`. **Failure mode:**
tidying the question up — the value is that it is in their words, typos
and all. **When to use:** search; a question typed the way people type it
is what platform search matches against.

### 17. Head To Head
**Shape:** {A} versus {B}. I ran both for {time}; one wasn't close.
**Example:** "Carousels versus reels. I ran both for 90 days and it was
not close." **On-screen:** `CAROUSELS vs REELS`. **Failure mode:**
refusing to pick — a comparison with a diplomatic ending is a video with
no ending. **When to use:** comments; people arrive already holding an
opinion, which is the point.

### 18. Cold Open Demo
**Shape:** start mid-action, already doing the thing. Watch what this
does. **Example:** "Watch what happens when I paste a competitor's caption
into this." **On-screen:** `WATCH THIS`. **Failure mode:** a result that
takes twelve seconds to arrive — if the payoff is slow, cut to it and show
the wait afterwards. **When to use:** screen recordings and anything with
a visible result; no setup, the hand is already moving.

### 19. The Deadline
**Shape:** {Thing} changes on {date}. Do {action} before then. **Example:**
"Your top nine grid crops change the second you post a reel. Fix the
covers first." **On-screen:** `BEFORE YOU POST AGAIN`. **Failure mode:**
manufactured urgency — one fake deadline and every future one is
discounted. **When to use:** urgency that is real: platform changes,
seasonal windows, policy updates.

### 20. Permission
**Shape:** You don't need {the thing everyone says you need}. **Example:**
"You do not need a niche. You need one person you can name."
**On-screen:** `YOU DON'T NEED A NICHE`. **Failure mode:** removing the
blocker and putting nothing in its place — the second half is the whole
video. **When to use:** relief; very high save rate because it removes a
blocker rather than adding a task.

### 21. Mid-Sentence Start
**Shape:** {...and that is when {the turn}.} **Example:** "...and that is
when the client asked for the money back. Three days after approving it."
**On-screen:** `HE ASKED FOR A REFUND`. **Failure mode:** never going back
to fill in the gap — deliver the setup by second eight or it reads as a
mistake. **When to use:** stories; starting mid-scene makes the viewer
feel they arrived late, which is a reason to stay.

### 22. Contrarian Flip
**Shape:** {Well-known saying, inverted.} **Example:** "Nice guys finish
first." **On-screen:** `NICE GUYS FINISH FIRST`. **Failure mode:** flipping
a saying nobody says — the original has to be a reflex, or the inversion is
just a sentence. **When to use:** comments; the viewer has to stop and
check whether they misheard you.

### 23. The Statistic
**Shape:** {N}% of {group} {surprising fact}. **Example:** "33% of high
school graduates never read another book." **On-screen:** `33% NEVER READ
AGAIN`. **Failure mode:** a statistic you cannot source — say where it
came from in the video or do not use it. **When to use:** sends; a number
a person can repeat at dinner gets sent to somebody.

### 24. The Reveal
**Shape:** This is the new {thing}. **Example:** "This is the new Sony
point and shoot, and it fixes the one thing I hated." **On-screen:** `THE
NEW ONE`. **Failure mode:** holding the thing up without saying what it
changes — a reveal with no stake is an advert. **When to use:** anything
physical or visual; the object is the hook and the words are captions on
it.

### 25. Someone Else's Result
**Shape:** There was one {person} who {outcome} in {time}. **Example:**
"There was one creator who grew a million subscribers in a single month."
**On-screen:** `1M SUBS IN ONE MONTH`. **Failure mode:** no name and no
link — an anonymous miracle is a rumour. **When to use:** proof when you
do not have your own number yet; third-person is allowed if it is true.

### 26. The Superlative
**Shape:** The {fastest/biggest/only} {thing} is {the answer}. **Example:**
"The fastest way to get confident is to build evidence you cannot argue
with." **On-screen:** `THE FASTEST WAY`. **Failure mode:** a superlative
with a soft answer — if the second half is "consistency", you have said
nothing and the comments will tell you. **When to use:** one-claim videos
with no story attached; the most common shape in the wild and the easiest
to write badly.

Scoring happens after selection: run every hook through the 5-property
panel (SPECIFICITY / ADDRESS / STAKES / CURIOSITY / BREVITY; verdict =
60% mean + 40% weakest property) before anyone wastes a take. See
`docs/WORKFLOWS.md` for the math and the worked example.
